1. Executive Summary (The Project in Plain English)

In any subscription business—especially telecommunications—churn represents the loss of existing customers who cancel their contracts or migrate to competitors. Acquiring a brand-new customer requires expensive marketing campaigns, sales commissions, onboarding hardware, and account setup costs. Research consistently confirms that retaining an existing subscriber costs 5 to 7 times less than acquiring a new one.

💡 The Healthcare Analogy (AI Doctor for Customer Accounts)
Think of this platform as an AI doctor for customer accounts:
  • The Diagnosis: Instead of subjective guessing, it inspects 20 concrete operational indicators (contract duration, monthly rate, tech support tickets, billing methods).
  • True Risk Calibration: Rather than a generic classifier score, it generates a mathematically calibrated probability (e.g., "This customer has a genuine 73.5% chance of leaving").
  • Root Cause Explanation (SHAP): It uncovers why the customer is unhappy (e.g., "Paying $85/month for Fiber Optic without Tech Support on a Month-to-Month plan").
  • The Prescription: It evaluates whether offering a $35 retention promotion yields a positive net return, protecting company funds from wasteful spending.

2. Problem Statement & Traditional Pitfalls

Traditional corporate machine learning models for customer churn fail in production due to four recurring flaws:

  1. The Arbitrary 0.5 Threshold: Treating any customer with a score > 0.5 as "churning" ignores the asymmetric economic costs of False Positives (spending money on someone who was never leaving) vs. False Negatives (losing a high-value $1,200/year customer).
  2. Uncalibrated Classifier Scores: Raw scores output by modern gradient boosting algorithms are ranking metrics, not real probabilities. Relying on uncalibrated scores to budget cash causes massive financial forecasting errors.
  3. Target Leakage in Pipelines: Computing data imputation or categorical encoding across the full dataset prior to train/test partitioning contaminates the test set, creating models that look stellar in notebooks but collapse in production.
  4. Whitespace & Data Quality Traps: The IBM Telco dataset contains 11 customer records with 0 tenure months where TotalCharges is stored as a blank space (" "). Without schema contracts, standard parsers crash or silently assign invalid values.

3. Core Project Objectives

  • Enforce Strict Data Contracts: Programmatic ingestion with SHA256 verification and Pandera schema validation.
  • Normalized Relational Architecture: 3NF PostgreSQL / SQLite storage for rapid analytical queries.
  • Leakage-Free Modeling: Strict scikit-learn ColumnTransformer and Pipeline encapsulation.
  • Probability Calibration: Platt Sigmoid Scaling to ensure model scores reflect true empirical likelihoods.
  • Cost-Sensitive Decision Boundaries: Finding the optimal operational threshold ($\tau^* = 0.540$) using cost-benefit optimization.
  • Transparent Explainable AI (SHAP): Global beeswarm drivers and individual customer waterfall attributions.
  • Financial Retention Simulator: 4-way data segregation tracking Observed, Predicted, Assumed, and Simulated metrics.
  • Production Microservices: Type-safe FastAPI REST API endpoints and a 5-tab executive Streamlit web dashboard.

6. Probability Calibration & Cost-Benefit Threshold Tuning

Platt Sigmoid Probability Calibration

Raw XGBoost outputs were calibrated using 5-fold cross-validated Platt scaling (CalibratedClassifierCV):

  • Brier Score: Reduced from 0.1610 to 0.1361 (-15.46% error reduction).
  • Log Loss: Dropped from 0.5283 to 0.4182 (-20.84% error reduction).
  • Expected Calibration Error (ECE): Improved by 68.3%, aligning scores directly with observed churn frequencies.

Cost-Benefit Decision Optimization

Assuming an intervention offer cost of $35.00, an assumed save rate of 22%, and a customer lifetime value (CLV) of $450.00:

Theoretical Bayes Threshold: tau* = Offer_Cost / (Save_Rate x CLV) = 35 / (0.22 x 450) = 0.3535
Cross-Validation Optimal Threshold: tau* = 0.540

8. Financial Retention Budget Simulator

To prevent counterfactual assumptions from being confused with recorded historical facts, all simulation tables enforce strict four-way column categorization:

  • [OBSERVED DATA]: Historic facts (tenure, billing rate, contract type).
  • [MODEL PREDICTION]: Calibrated probability and risk tier.
  • [HYPOTHETICAL ASSUMPTION]: Offer discount cost, campaign budget, assumed retention success rate.
  • [SIMULATED OUTCOME]: Expected gross revenue saved, net profit, campaign ROI, and target ranking.
